Our History, Heritage & Soul

Preserving 56 years of sacred South Indian culinary craftsmanship.

The Genesis

A Small Copper Vessel & An Unyielding Faith

In October 1968, late Shri Krishnamurthy Iyer lit the first brass lamp at a humble six-table tiffin parlor under the shadow of the Mylapore Kapaleeshwarar temple.

He brought with him no financial riches, but three sacred things: an heirloom brass coffee decoction filter handed down from his grandmother, seasoned black stone wet grinders from Thanjavur, and an unshakeable belief that "Annadhanam"—the sharing of pure, lovingly prepared nourishment—is the highest form of worship.

Today, over five decades later, the morning queue outside our doors still begins before sunrise. The world has changed; our stone grinding cycles, single-origin spices, and open-hearted smiles have not.

Milestones

A Journey Through Half A Century

Key chapters in our five-decade dedication to authentic Southern hospitality.

1968

The First Lamp in Mylapore

Shri Krishnamurthy Iyer inaugurates a humble 6-table shop offering hot idli, medu vada, and filter coffee brewed with freshly ground Chicory beans.

1982

The Seasoned Cast-Iron Era

Introduction of the 4-foot seasoned heavy iron tawa, pioneering the famous paper-thin Ghee Roast Dosa that drew food pilgrims from across Tamil Nadu.

1999

Traditional Kalyana Sapadu Catering

Launched our dedicated wedding feast division, bringing 32-course authentic banana leaf feasts to high-profile traditional weddings.

2014

Direct Western Ghats Farming Co-ops

Formed direct procurement partnerships with smallholder spice farmers in Idukki, Salem, and Guntur, guaranteeing unadulterated spices.

2026

Contemporary Gastronomy & Global Acclaim

Unveiling Taste of South Atelier — celebrating modern culinary presentation without relinquishing a single nuance of our sacred wet-stone fermentation.

Principles

The Four Pillars of Our Philosophy

Natural Fermentation

Zero chemical yeasts. Natural air-borne lactobacillus fermentation for exactly 14 hours in stone urns.

Pure A2 Desi Cow Ghee

Sourced exclusively from indigenous cow breeds, churned via traditional wooden bilona methods.

Soapstone & Iron Cooking

We cook our sambars, rasams, and vatha kuzhambu only in natural soapstone kalchattis for alkaline balance.

Satvik Integrity

Clean, calming, nourishing food that leaves the body light, energized, and deeply peaceful.

Heritage & Roots

Frequently Asked Questions

Discover the heritage, traditional cookware, and timeless principles behind our 58-year journey.

Taste of South was established in October 1968 by late Shri Krishnamurthy Iyer near the sacred Kapaleeshwarar Temple tank in Mylapore. Beginning with just six wooden tables and a single copper vessel, our founder’s vow was simple: serve food with the same devotion, purity, and sacred warmth as offered in a traditional home kitchen.

Hand-carved Kalchatti soapstone vessels retain steady, gentle alkaline heat that prevents delicate spice oils from scorching, naturally balancing the acidity in our Vathal Kuzhambu and Rasam. Meanwhile, our 40-year seasoned heavy cast iron tawas deliver the incomparable golden, honeycomb crust unique to authentic Tamil dosas.

We bypass commercial wholesale brokers to work directly with ancestral farming estates across South India: Tellicherry bold black pepper from the misty slopes of Wayanad, GI-tagged turmeric roots from Salem, green cardamom from Idukki, cold-pressed sesame oil from Chettinad, and unpolished Gundu chillies from Ramanathapuram.

We follow the handwritten culinary notebooks left by our founding master cooks. Every masala powder is roasted and pounded in-house weekly in measured small batches. Senior chefs apprentice for five years in our kitchen before being permitted to head the master sambar and ghee roast stations.
